# Academic and official evidence (2026-11-09)

Written for: the research team. Read depth is stated for every source. "Snippet" means only a search-result summary was seen; "Abstract" means the abstract page was seen; "Not read" means the source was identified but its content was not reviewed. No finding below is stated beyond what the accessible text supports.

**Overall assessment for Indian equity momentum:** several Indian studies report momentum profits, and at least two say they survive costs (A1, A2 described in snippets). None was read in full. Sample periods end before the current project data window. Survivorship bias and the absence of point-in-time constituent lists are not addressed in any source seen. **These sources are evidence of a hypothesis, not of an edge.**

## B. Index derivatives evidence

| # | Source | Date | Read depth | Finding relied on | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Slivka, Liang and Xue, "An Empirical Study of Nifty 50 Option Time Spreads", *Indian Journal of Finance* (IJF) | 2020 (in-sample 2015–2019; out-of-sample) | Abstract and listing (search result) | Four rules for Nifty 50 call calendar spreads built from near-the-money pricing; reported as a starting point, not a finished system | Calendar spreads (defined debit risk); full text not read; option prices for 2015–2019 are outside the project's data window |
| B2 | Kumar, University of Vaasa master's thesis, delta-neutral Nifty 50 option strategies (straddles etc.) | 2025 | Search-result summary | India VIX has little effect on performance in the thesis's sample; Nifty treated as semi-efficient | Master's thesis; limited sample; not peer-reviewed |
| B3 | Nifty spread study, 2007–2018, monthly data (bull call and bull put profitable; bear call and bear put loss-making) | Undated | Secondary summary (earlier session) | Bull spreads profitable, bear spreads loss-making over 2007–2018 | Index points, not per-trade returns on capital; costs unknown; full paper not located |
| B4 | Durgia, weekly Nifty option selling | 2025 | Search-result summary | Argues for systematic weekly selling with risk controls | Undefined-risk selling; not peer-reviewed; not relied on |
| B5 | Commercial backtests (Share.market, Strike.money, Scribd, TradingView ideas) | Various | Search results | Short-strangle, ratio-spread and calendar backtests | Broker or blog material; unverified; not relied on |

**Overall assessment for index options:** the closest peer-reviewed-style evidence is B1 (calendar spreads) and B3 (bull and bear debit spreads, secondary). Both are abstract- or summary-level, both precede the project's option-price window, and neither reports bid/ask fills. **No source in this phase supports a directional debit spread edge on NIFTY, BANKNIFTY, FINNIFTY, MIDCPNIFTY or other index derivatives after realistic costs.**

## C. Stock derivatives and covered-call evidence

| # | Source | Date | Read depth | Finding relied on | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Misra and Dalmia, covered call and protective put on the Nifty (*Management Dynamics*, vol 7 iss 2) | 2007 | Snippet (earlier session) | Both hedged portfolios reported lower risk than the unhedged index; covered call reported higher average return | Full text not read; sample period not confirmed |
| C2 | Aggarwal, covered call on the S&P CNX Nifty (*Asia-Pacific Business Review*) | 2011 | Secondary summary | 5% in-the-money covered call reported to beat the index on standard measures, Oct 2005–Sep 2010 | Full text not located; index-level, not stock-level |
| C3 | Asian Journal of Finance and Accounting | 2014 | Secondary summary | 55 NSE F&O stocks; protective puts outperformed covered calls overall | Full text not read |
| C4 | NSE research paper comppaper195 (buy-and-hold Nifty futures vs covered calls, VaR) | Undated | Snippet | Rolling covered calls reported to yield higher returns for higher risk | Full text not read |
| C5 | CBOE collar benchmark (95–110 collar index) and Whaley BXM work | 2002–2012 | Secondary summaries | US benchmark definitions and results | US market; not Indian |
| C6 | SEBI study, F&O profit and loss of individual traders, FY22–FY24 (SEBI press release 22/2024, September 2024) | Sep 2024 | Press coverage only; primary report not opened | Press coverage reports that about 91% of individual traders lost money in FY24 and about 93% over FY22–FY24 (one set of figures in the search results gives 92.8% over the period); the earlier January 2023 study reported 89% for FY22 | Primary PDF not opened; loss figures include transaction costs; these are descriptive loss-rate figures, not a test of any strategy |

**Overall assessment for covered calls and collars:** Indian covered-call evidence (C1–C4) is positive for risk reduction and modest return enhancement in the reported samples, but none was read in full and the samples are old. Collars have no Indian evidence found. The SEBI statistics (C6) show that most individual F&O traders lost money; they are not evidence about any particular strategy, and the primary report should be read before any use.
