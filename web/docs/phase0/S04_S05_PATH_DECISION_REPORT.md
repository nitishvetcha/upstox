# S-04 / S-05 decision report after path simulation (SYNTHETIC, research only)

**No stop or target formula is selected. Production formulas, Strategy 11.4, weights, thresholds and risk limits are unchanged. Engine: NO-GO. No historical or holdout data was used.**

Evidence: `PATH_SIMULATION_RESULTS.md` (generated by `scripts/phase3-path-simulation.ts`). Every table in that file carries the synthetic-only limitation.

## 1. Current production implementation and constants
Spread: long call near delta +0.5, short call at long strike + width. Width = ceil(daily ATR / step) × step (ATR 180, step 50 → 200). Stop = 0.5 × debit. Target 1 = debit + 0.5 × max profit. Target 2 = debit + 0.8 × max profit. Hard-coded: 0.5, 0.5, 0.8, and the width rule. The documentation does not derive any of them.

## 2. Mathematical explanation
Spread value V = C(S, K1, σ, t) − C(S, K2, σ, t). Debit D = V at entry (plus slippage). Max profit = width − D. Max loss = D (plus costs). A stop or target is a level on V. Each leg is repriced at every step.

## 3. Method (what changed from the terminal test)
- Intraday paths of 75 five-minute steps, sub-sampled 4×; both legs repriced at each sub-step.
- Stop and target checked at every sub-step. Where both could be inside one sub-step, the stop is assumed first.
- Stop-outs that recover before the close are recorded as exits.
- Path families: trend up, trend down, reversal (down then recover), sideways, shock then partial recovery, gap down then trend up; plus four combinations with IV changes (underlying up with IV falling, down with IV rising, sideways with IV rising, shock with IV spike).
- Costs: base 1× with no added slippage; adverse 1.5× with 2% slippage per leg per side; stress 2× with 4%.

## 5. Failure cases
- **Stop then recovery:** in reversal and gap families, stops fire and the position would have recovered. Recorded as exits (column "Stop then recovered").
- **Gap through stop:** a 1.2-ATR gap breaches the 50% stop; these are recorded as STOP_GAP at the gap level, not the stop level.
- **Theta-only losses:** in flat-IV, sideways and falling-IV families, losses come from decay alone.
- **Targets rarely filled:** the 0.8-of-max-profit target is hit in few paths; its averages come mostly from the time exit.
- **Scenario-based levels** depend on assumed volatility and time; if the real IV differs, the levels are wrong.
- **Width and debit:** at 200 points the debit is about 75 per unit, against a max profit of about 125. Each lot costs about ₹4,900 at risk, which is far above the ₹1,000 per-trade budget at ₹1 lakh capital. The sizing rule therefore allows zero lots at default capital. Narrower widths lower the debit but also the profit; the trade-off is a research question.

## 6. Execution and cost sensitivity
- Slippage is applied once per leg per side on mid; it is not added on top of bid/ask. Bid and ask depth are not observed, so the 2% and 4% levels are assumptions.
- Going from base to stress lowers the average P&L by about 8.7 points per unit for the current formulas (−12.8 to −21.4). That combines the 2x cost multiplier with 4% slippage per leg per side. Brokerage, exchange, STT, stamp duty and GST are included.
- The approved scenario multipliers are sensitivity assumptions, not verified schedules.

## 7. Candidate assessment (no winner)
- **Current formulas:** negative on average under all three cost scenarios in these families. Do not keep as validated.
- **Tighter stops (30–70% of debit):** change the result most. Which one is better depends on how often paths reverse; the synthetic families are my choice, so this is a bias risk.
- **Scenario-based levels:** interpretable, but they depend on the volatility assumption and perform worst here.
- **Recommended research candidates to carry forward:** (a) stop at a fraction of debit tested across a wider and independently chosen set of path families, and (b) a target at full repricing of both legs. Both need independent market observations before any weight is given to them.

## 8. Things this report does not show
- Win probability, expectancy or profitability. These are synthetic model results.
- Whether any formula works on real markets. That needs option quotes, IV history, bid/ask depth and expiry behaviour (all BLOCKED).
- That the family weights are right. The average above is unweighted, and a different weighting can change the ranking.
