## 2. Is the daily-ATR width rule appropriate?
Open research question, not decided here. The rule ties width to the daily range, so debit exposure grows on volatile days. Whether an alternative width method (for example, a fixed number of strikes, or a delta-based short strike) is better is research for a later phase.

## 3. Expanded path simulation (equal-weight primary diagnostic; family weights are not probabilities)
- 23 families across five categories; 40 paths per family; 75 steps sub-sampled 4×; both legs repriced at every sub-step; stops and targets checked at every sub-step; stop assumed first when both fall in one sub-step.
- Separate development and sensitivity seed samples. The development-sample and sensitivity-sample means for the current stop and target differ by about 0.1 point, so the result is not seed-driven.

**Ranking instability (key result):** the top candidate changes with the cost scenario and with the weighting. At width 200, base costs:
- S1 (stop 30%) + T2 (target at full repricing of +1 ATR): equal-weight mean +1.0, stress-weighted +2.7.
- The same pair under adverse costs: −5.8. Under stress: −11.3.
- At width 50 and width 100, the same pair is negative under adverse and stress costs.
So the pair that looks best under base costs is not robust to costs. No candidate is positive across all cost scenarios.

Other points:
- The current formulas (S0 with T0 or T1) are negative on average under every cost scenario and at every width tested.
- Under stress costs, the equal-weight best candidate is S2 (stop 70%) with T2. The stress-weighted best is S1 with T2. Weighting changes the answer.
- Stop-then-recovery is common (about 22% of paths at width 200, base). It is recorded as an exit, as required.
- Gap-through-stop is rare in these families (under 1%).

## 4. Cost and execution sensitivity
- Going from base to stress costs lowers the average P&L per unit by about 8 to 14 points at width 200, depending on the candidate (verified: 7.6 to 14.1).
- Legging sensitivity (extra 1% adverse fill on the second entry leg) reduces the best candidate's average by a few tenths at width 200 and more at narrower widths.
- Partial fills, market depth and real bid-ask spreads are NOT TESTED. No depth data exists.
